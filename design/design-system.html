Trail design system

Visual source of truth for built design. Unbuilt design stages in proposals.

How this sheet works

One section per foundation or component, each a boxed demo with a one-paragraph annotation stating the law it carries. Foundations first (Color, Type, Spacing, Shape and elevation, Motion, Voice), then the state grammar, then components. One home per component: extend a section in place rather than adding a duplicate. Every demo must render correctly in both themes (the toggle above), and conformance against this sheet means computed styles and interaction, not vibes. Components reference tokens only; if a value is not a token, it does not ship.

Color

The palette is charcoal ink, electric blue, and cool sparse neutrals. One gray ramp carries the whole interface, and the step number encodes the job: 100 to 300 are tinted fills and hovers, 400 to 600 are borders, 700 to 800 are muted foreground, 900 to 1000 are text. Rank information with the ramp before reaching for a hue. Meaning-color is rationed to three hues, each with exactly one meaning: blue is the product's hand (primary action, selection, links) and the Ready call awaiting the operator's move; green means ratified — Done and nothing else; amber means the ball is in your court — Waiting and nothing else. Liveness is signaled by motion, not a hue (see Motion). State is never signaled by color alone. Palette expansion is a maintainer-only ruling; propose, never add.

Type

Two working families plus one signature: the sans for everything, the mono for code, paths, and identifiers, and Roboto Serif for the wordmark alone (D22) — the brand role is the largest type on screen, outranking the page title, and no other element may take the serif. The ruled product faces are Roboto (neo-grotesque) and Roboto Mono; the brand face Apercu is commercial and never bundled. Helvetica, the brand site's own fallback, backs the sans. The brand's defining type law: headings keep weight 400; hierarchy comes from size and color, never bold. Weight 500 exists for labels and controls only — at most those two weights per view. Each role sets size, weight, line-height, and tracking together as one token; components apply a role, never hand-set properties. A component's own section may spec a size variant of a role (the badge and the small button do); a new family or weight is a ruling, not a variant.

Spacing

A 4px base scale, and a rhythm: 8 inside a group, 16 between groups, 32 between sections. Full cards pad 24; compact cards (task cards) pad 12/16. Tree indentation steps by 16 per level. If a gap is not on the scale, the layout is guessing.

Shape and elevation

Two radii plus the pill: 6 for controls, inputs, and chips, 12 for cards and menus. Never mix rounded and sharp corners in one view. Elevation is a ramp of layered low-alpha shadows: raised for cards, float for menus and popovers. A tier beyond float is a ruling.

Iconography

One glyph source for every icon: Material Symbols Outlined (wght 500, 24px grid; D22), official path data inlined as symbols, rendered filled in a 12px box on currentColor — icons inherit the text color of whatever carries them, never set their own. The set stays minimal and purposeful; an icon exists only where a glyph disambiguates faster than a word (chips, tree carets). Decorative icons do not ship. A new icon is picked from the same Material set at the same weight and lands in this one library; ids stay semantic (what the icon means here, not Material's glyph name). Selection rides the fill axis: the selected carrier — the active nav destination — swaps its icons to the FILL-1 variant; hover and focus keep the outline form, and glyphs whose filled form is identical simply hold. The hue never changes with the swap: icons keep inheriting currentColor.

Motion

Nothing animates by default; motion exists to clarify a change, never to decorate. When it earns its place: one easing, three durations (150ms state changes, 200ms reveals, 300ms overlays), and reduced-motion preferences are always honored. Motion is Trail's liveness signal: the continuous animations in the product are the Running indicator's breathing dot (--duration-live, one calm 2s breath) — a task moving without the operator's hand — and, on the same breath, skeleton placeholders while content loads (dogfood r4.1): loading is genuinely live, so it may move; shape-matched .skel blocks stand where the content will land and the container keeps its settled size, so nothing jumps. Everything settled is still; stillness is how settled states read as settled.

Voice & microcopy

This section is law for every string the product ships. Sheet prose and internal docs stay normal writing; user-authored and agent-authored content is not app copy and is out of scope.

  • Say less. Helper text only when the affordance cannot speak for itself; default none.
  • Calm register. Sentence case everywhere; no all-caps labels; no urgency theatrics; state the thing, do not perform it.
  • Controls say what they do. Verb plus object ("Dispatch", "Connect to agents"), the same name through the whole flow; never "OK" or "Submit".
  • Errors direct, never apologize. What happened plus the next step; an empty state points to the first action.
  • Empty states carry good news calmly (dogfood r4): "Nothing waiting", "Nothing yet" — state the clear queue plainly and stop; never negate the operator ("Nothing needs you" died here). One ruled exception (D26): the Inbox's both-sections-empty state is the product's win condition and gets the one celebratory moment — the full-height hero with the celebration glyph and "You are all caught up!"; nothing else in the product exclaims.
  • Status labels are fixed: Planned, Running, Waiting, Ready, Done, Shelved — from one source module in code; no view invents status strings.
  • Done is never a claim. Copy states evidence plainly ("No artifacts attached"), never celebrates, never soothes; absence of proof is said out loud.
  • Demo data declares itself in UI copy when a seeded snapshot is served; it is never dressed as live. (Built: the Demo declarations section owns the marks.)
  • No em dashes and no semicolons in product copy (maintainer, 2026-07-16, semicolons added the same day's content review): punctuate with periods, colons, commas, or parentheses; the dash and the semicolon stay docs-register marks. Swept examples: "Dispatched. Session starting", "Commit 9c2e1d4: subject", "None (life-level)", "Only the project will be deleted. 17 tasks will stay in the backlog, unassigned."
  • Confirmations speak in the future (maintainer, 2026-07-16): a confirm dialog names what will happen on confirm — "will be deleted", "will stay" — never present tense, which reads as already done.

Status grammar

The one home for task states; every other section points here instead of re-specifying. Six states, one field: Planned, Running, Waiting, Ready, Done, Shelved. Rulings carried: liveness is motion on gray, not a hue (Running breathes); amber means the ball is in your court (Waiting); blue marks the operator's decision point (Ready, awaiting their move); green means ratified (Done) — and only a human moves anything to Done. Badges are pills with a dot and a sentence-case word so state never rides on color alone; a badge is never button-shaped. On the board, the column carries the state and cards stay clean; badges appear where no column says it (backlog rows, task detail).

Planned Running Waiting Ready Done Shelved
  • Planned: gray, static. Running: gray with the breathing dot — the product's only continuous motion. Waiting: amber. Ready: blue. Done: green dot. Shelved: outline, muted.
  • Done is a manual operator move — the detail's status field or a board drag; manual moves are never blocked into any state (D11); no automation ever sets Done, and automation's ceiling is Ready.
  • Reduced motion: the breathing dot freezes at full opacity; the word still carries the state.
  • Labels come from the one source module (see Voice); a status badge is never a click target.

Controls

Full component, all variants and every state in one demo, spec notes underneath. Primary is the only brand-filled control on a surface; everything else ranks by the gray ramp.

  • Heights: 36px default, 30px small; label role type; radius --radius-s.
  • Hover and press step along the ramp (gray-100, gray-200; border 400 to 500); primary darkens to --brand-hover.
  • Focus lights the component's border (ruled 2026-07-15, dogfood r2, supersedes the two-layer ring): one global rule — a 1px brand outline hugging the edge (outline-offset: -1px) on :focus-visible, every focusable thing, both themes; never an outer ring, halo, or second box. Brand-filled carriers (primary buttons) light in --brand-ink. No component writes its own focus rule.
  • Disabled is the gray-100 fill with gray-700 text, never an opacity trick.

Chips

Small metadata carriers: mono type, radius-s, an icon from the library where the kind needs disambiguating. The MVP set is two kinds, one shape: the project pill (folder icon — the task's working directory; renamed from workspace chip 2026-07-15) and the artifact chip (commit, pull request, or session icon — one output with a source pointer). Artifact chips live in the task detail only; list and board views never show them (ruled 2026-07-14). An artifact chip is a click target: it opens the source. Further chip kinds (rollover provenance and others) are deferred until the maintainer rules them needed.

  • Chips are --type-data mono on gray-100 with a gray-300 border; hover steps the ramp only when clickable.
  • The pill label caps at 16ch and ellipsizes; the full name rides the native title (the truncation rule, Type section). The icon never shrinks (ruled 2026-07-15, dogfood r1B).
  • The icon names the kind, so the text can stay short (a SHA, a PR number, a duration).

Popovers

One primitive owns every float panel — menus, pickers, the calendar (converged 2026-07-15, dogfood r1B; Popover.tsx in the build). The panel portals to the document body and positions fixed from its trigger's viewport rect, so opening one never reflows an ancestor and never adds a scrollbar to the page or any container. It appears below the trigger, start-aligned, one --space-2 gap; when the space below cannot hold it and above has more, it flips above; at a horizontal viewport edge it mirrors its alignment, then clamps. Content past the height cap scrolls inside the panel, never the page. Escape dismisses only the top layer — a menu inside a modal closes and leaves the modal open — and returns focus to the trigger; an outside pointer-down closes and leaves focus where the user clicked. Call sites own only trigger state and content; what the panel looks like stays with its owning section (menus, calendar).

Project
  • Placement: below the trigger, start-aligned, gap --space-2, 8px collision padding against the viewport; flips above when below cannot hold the panel and above has more room; mirrors its alignment then clamps at a horizontal edge.
  • Height: min(20rem, available space on the chosen side); longer content scrolls inside the panel — the page never gains scrollbars.
  • Dismissal: outside pointer-down and Escape both close; Escape acts on the top layer only and hands focus back to the trigger; an outside click leaves focus where the user clicked.
  • An open panel tracks its trigger: position recomputes on any scroll outside the panel, window resize, and panel-content resize. Scrolling inside the panel never re-places it — the trigger hasn't moved, and re-measuring mid-scroll would snap the content back to the top.
  • Layering: the panel floats above the modal layer. The literal z-indexes (50 over the modal's 40) are unruled — a z-scale is an open maintainer question; mint no new z values meanwhile.
  • Containers never pre-clip for panels: the backlog tree and lists carry no overflow: hidden — corners come from radii on the head and last row — so a clipped ancestor can neither swallow a panel nor collapse under the shell grid (r1B).
